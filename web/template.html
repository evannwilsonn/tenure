      Where to draw the line

      The model gives a probability. Someone still has to decide how high is high enough to call the customer. Lower the line and you catch more leavers, but you also phone more people who were staying anyway. These figures come from the 1,409 test customers the model never saw.

      Why not just use accuracy? About 27% of customers leave, so a model that predicts "nobody leaves" is 73% accurate and useless. F1 balances precision and recall. That's why the line was set where cross-validated F1 peaked, not left at 50%.

      What moved the score. At a 50% line the model caught about half the leavers (F1 ). Moving the line down to did most of the work. Weighting churners more heavily did something similar, but less well. Adding log-tenure, a count of add-ons and a "first six months" flag helped a little.

      Trees didn't win. ML.NET's gradient-boosted trees scored F1 on the test set with their own tuned line, just under the logistic model. The logistic model is also the one whose reasons you can read straight off, so it's the one deployed.
